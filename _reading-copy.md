# 3. THE OPENING DECISION

**I led with the SHELF/one-place angle — the builder's own.** The lede's first sentence is the "thousand websites" idea stated as fact rather than complaint: six systems, six places, now one.

Why it beat the others here: this page inherits a reader who was already told *six of them are already yours*. The door did the wanting. What the page owes is not a new reason to want — it is the relief of not having to go and assemble six scattered readings and reconcile them yourself. That is a felt cost, not a doubt, so naming it doesn't trip the defensiveness rule the way D4's full "Read together" section did.

But I did **not** take D4's execution. D4 wrote the pain first and the room second — a paragraph on how mute and scattered the six systems are elsewhere, then the relief. That is objection-handling in a calm voice, and it works on the reader. So the shelf angle survives as **one clause of fact**, and the warmth is carried by D7's second person, which is the register's actual answer to "steady and warm, not quiet and distant."

The runners-up were rejected for this page specifically: **D2 (classical)** owns the best sentences in the set but its two strongest — "looking for it is just looking" and "filed under the date" — are both **false** against the engine (see §5). **D1 (the old ways)** front-loads credibility the door already bought. **D3 (anchoring)** lifts itself by calling the reader's sun sign placemat-grade.

# 5. WHAT I CUT AND WHY

**"Nothing is drawn. Each mark is looked up in the archive and read as it stands."** — *Beautiful and untrue.* `arcana-reading.js:117-119`: only sun sign, year animal and life path are derived from the date. Rune, trigram and hexagram are `pick(list, seed+"f"/"g"/"h")` — an FNV-1a hash draw off the `birth~name~y~m~d` seed. Deterministic, yes; a lookup, no. Replaced with what determinism actually buys: *nothing is rolled and nothing is re-rolled on re-view.* The eyebrow's "given, not drawn" became "given, not chosen" for the same reason — one word, and it is true of all six.

**"The same DATE returns the same six."** — False, and falsifiable in ten seconds by any reader who enters a nickname. The seed is name + date. Every instance now reads *the same name and the same date.* **This correction is also owed to the door and to the intake screen.**

**"A date of birth. Nothing else."** — False; the intake asks for the name first (`arcane.js:279`, "The name borne"; `app.js:1476` CTA is literally "Give a name and date"). It was the page's most exposed line — contradicted by the very next screen. Now "A name and a date of birth."

**"Life path — 9 in the archive."** — Wrong count. `codex.html` holds `nu-01`…`nu-12`, and `reduceNum()` explicitly stops at 11, 22, 33. It is **12**, and the archive is one click away.

**"Out of the six comes one crowned name."** — Overclaim. `crownOf()` uses sun + year animal (→ epithet), rune (→ role), trigram (→ binding). Life path and hexagram contribute nothing; `fragment()` returns `null` for both — the build itself cannot say what they did. Rewritten to four build it, two stand beside it, which is both true and a more interesting sentence.

**"It sits on your shelf, whole, and you can open it again in a year."** — *Beautiful and untrue.* There is no reading store. The only persistence is a boolean (`arcane.js:1205`, `br_has_reading`), and `br_holdings` is labelled in the code as "the MOCK localStorage flag." What actually reopens a reading is the seed in the URL. Replaced with the engine's own honest line: the link is the receipt. Say the shelf when the shelf exists.

**"as the archive is written further, the thing you already own opens further with it… and in time the panels."** — The panels are filed under *"Drawn, not built"* in `SURFACE_BRIEF_V1.md` §2.4 and were on the page as settled present tense. Kept the retention idea, moved to a tense the house can stand behind.

**D2's "each mark was already sitting where you'd find it, filed under the date, and looking for it is just looking."** — The best sentence in the entire fleet, and I cut it. Nothing is filed under a date, and half the marks are not sitting anywhere until a hash picks them. It is the exact false claim above, written better. **Do not revive it without rebuilding the three hash-picked marks as real lookups — at which point it is the first line you should reach for.**

**D2's "This one doesn't." / "Most of what you're told about yourself goes between the hearing and the forgetting."** — The first is a competitor rebuttal with an edge; the second manufactures a small grief so the shelf can relieve it. Both work on the reader.

**D3's "the kind of thing that turns up on a placemat."** — Corrects the reader's taste, then sells the upgrade.

**D5's "none of them made for you" (headline) and "not composed to flatter you."** — The headline chills on first parse, which is expensive in the H1 slot; the second rebuts an accusation nobody made and plants the idea that readings flatter. "Nothing here is generated" was also cut as flatly false — the crowned name is string composition at runtime.

**D4's "nothing here is left for you to gather up afterward."** — Reassurance-by-denial, exactly as PART 0 defines it.
