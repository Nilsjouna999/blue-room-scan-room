# U1 — THE CONCEPT, POLISHED  *(superseded — see the banner above)*

## THE ONE LINE

**A dark machined plate you scroll down, where six marks are derived from one stranger's name and birthday with every step shown, while a sheet of paper laid beside it fills with the reading those marks make — one wet edge crossing both — and at the floor the plate and the sheet fold together into a record you could keep.**

The spine is **Proposal 1 (checkable)**, because it keeps five of the six words in the builder's own sentence and gives up only "elegantly." Everything grafted below exists to buy that word back.

---

## WHAT IT PORTRAYS

A stranger scrolls once and leaves able to say three things without being told: *it starts from almost nothing — a name and a date*; *the marks are worked out, not made up, because I watched the sums*; *and what you get at the end is a filed thing with my name on it.*

The argument is a **conversion**. Left of the seam is what the calendar and the name hand over — unarguable, arithmetic, cold. Right of the seam is what the house makes of it — warm, written, interpretive. The surface never says "given, not chosen"; it shows a sum resolving on one material at the same instant a sentence lands on the other. That simultaneity is the entire product, performed.

---

## THE FOUR BEATS, AS DECIDED

**BEAT 1 — THE GIVEN.**
*Shows:* `JOHN BON · 26 · 07 · 1965`, set on the plate at its head, and nothing else. The paper sheet is present but blank. The wax seal sits unstamped at the plate's foot, visible from the first frame.
*How:* No motion. The three planes and the one keylight establish themselves while nothing runs.
*Proves:* How little the machine is given. A premise that lingers becomes a title card, so it is short.

**BEAT 2 — THE FILL.**
*Shows:* Six slots cut through the plate. Each fills with one line of working and one result. Critically — and this is the synthesis's own finding, which no proposal caught — **the six divide into two threes, because the engine does**:

- Slots 1–3 (sun, animal, life path) are **deterministic from the birth date** and their working is arithmetic the reader can redo with a pencil: `26 JUL · JUL 23 – AUG 22 → LEO`; `1965 → SNAKE`; `2+6+0+7+1+9+6+5 = 36 → 3+6 = 9`.
- Slots 4–6 (rune, trigram, hexagram) are **drawn from the seed by hash** — `pick(by.rune, seed+"f")` — and their working is not a sum, it is a **draw notation**: `birth~John~1965~7~26 → #19 of 24 → URUZ`.

That is not a compromise; it is the honest shape of the engine, and it rescues the design from the "worksheet" charge. Three lines of arithmetic and three lines of draw notation read as a *machine's two operations*, not as six identical homework cells.

*How:* The working feeds **upward from behind the plate**, seen through the slot, and comes to rest at the slot's lip. A thin bright meniscus rides the slot as the digits land. One line per mark, never two.
*Proves:* The marks are derived. Checkably for three of them; visibly-drawn-from-a-fixed-deck for the other three.

**BEAT 3 — THE LETTER, IN THE SAME FRAME.**
*Shows:* Six passages of unaddressed prose on the paper sheet, each landing as its slot's result reaches the lip.
*How:* Per-mark settle with a short per-line order inside the passage. Ink arrives at full density and is **unmasked left-to-right**, never faded up from zero. The same meniscus crosses the sheet, offset in phase, so a reader taking in the whole frame sees one wet front moving through two materials.
*Proves:* Translation. One scalar, two surfaces, one visible edge — the meniscus is the shared clock made physical, and it is the single best organ in the six proposals (grafted from **Proposal 4, darkroom**).

**BEAT 4 — THE TRAVEL AND THE FOLD.**
*Shows:* The six lit lips detach from the plate as tiles. The sheet folds once along a single seam. All of it travels **up through a fixed opaque foreground band** — the Codex's tide, reused — and resurfaces below as one sealed record: the two givens at its head, the six marks down its spine still carrying their condensed derivations, the seal stamped. One way in.
*How:* One continuous movement, no crossfade. Identity survives the trip: the thing that arrives is visibly the thing that left. One gleam crosses the fold line as it closes, and then nothing moves.
*Proves:* The house delivers an object, not an opinion.

## THE FOUR QUESTIONS, ANSWERED

**Q1 — SHOW THE WORKING. One line per mark, in two kinds.**
*Reason:* The site's central claim is checkable or it is a horoscope, and U1 is the only surface whose job is to make it checkable. *Runner-up beaten:* result-only (**Proposal 6**), which the builder-sentence critic correctly convicted of dropping "translated" — with no visible derivation there is nothing on screen for the letter to be a translation **of**, leaving only synchronised timing. *Discipline against the worksheet charge:* monospace, fixed slot width set by the longest string up front (so no layout thrash and a trivial static fallback), working set smaller and dimmer than its result, and the two-kinds split above.

*A blocker the critics raised and I am dissolving:* Proposal 1 held that showing the working blocks on the rune/trigram engine bug. It does not. That defect is a **correlation across seeds** — the rune determines the trigram over the population. On a single fixture, with one seed, no reader can see it. The bug stays real and stays queued; it does not gate this surface.

**Q2 — THE WRITTEN PAGE. Unaddressed, unsigned, no hand-script face. Per-mark settle.**
*Reason:* An addressed, signed letter is one bad sentence from the psychic's letter, and the underwhelm critic is right that it sits nearest the exact "trashy" costume the last build was named for. Worse, its quality is unmeasurable by anyone in this loop. *Runner-up beaten:* **Proposal 2 (letter)** — which the builder-sentence critic caught quietly trading "at the same time" for "within a shared window." *What I kept from it anyway:* its paper as a genuine material, and its fold-into-record as Beat 4. A letter, when finished, is folded — the metaphor performs the transform for free, which is what "sleekly" means. *Why not stroke-by-stroke:* typing is unreadable at fast scrub and grotesque in reverse, and the scroll-is-the-clock ruling requires reversibility at every frame.

**Q3 — A RECORD, WITH A WAY TO OPEN IT.**
*Reason:* Ending on the letter shows the house thinking; ending on a filed object shows it delivering, which is the register. It also matches every other product in the house — the card is a record with a link, a codex entry is a record with a link. *Runner-up beaten:* the reading itself, which repeats Beat 3 and ends on text the reader has already read. *Non-negotiable:* the record's spine still carries `= 36 → 9`. A record that only says LIFE PATH 9 threw away the thing the surface just taught.

## MATERIAL AND DEPTH

The scrapped build was a cream rectangle because it had **one plane and no light**. This is three planes, two materials, and exactly one lamp.

**One keylight, fixed.** A single radial source high and slightly left of centre, established in Beat 1 and never moved. Every highlight in the descent sits on the same side. No per-box glow, no per-section background. This alone is most of the distance between "object in a room" and "div with a shadow."

**Plane B — the plate.** Near-black, a three-stop vertical gradient not a fill, a 1px raking highlight along its top edge, an engraved hairline inset ~9px from the edge, `corner-shape: bevel`, registration ticks at the corners. The six boxes are not drawn on it; they are **slots cut through it**.

**Plane A — behind the plate.** Where the arithmetic lives, genuinely occluded. Working feeds upward and is physically clipped by the slot's lip. Luminance obeys the Accord's transmission law: deepest in the aperture the digits are dimmest and coolest; at the lip the result is brightest and warmest, because the eye is looking through less material. This is the difference between depth and two font sizes.

**Plane C — glass, flush with the viewport.** Opacity ~.012, a single diagonal gleam per beat that dies before 45% across, a faint corner vignette. A second gleam turns glass back into gradient.

**The second material — the sheet.** Warm, matte, `feTurbulence` grain at overlay ~0.2 (the same technique the card already uses), a hairline deckle on its outer edge, a dual shadow — 2px near-black contact plus 40px ambient — and **set 0.4° off-axis from the page grid, fixed, not a wobble.** A sheet laid by a hand is never square to the desk. Its inner edge **overlaps the plate's outer edge**, so z-order is honest and provable.

**That off-axis, asymmetric overlap is the deliberate answer to the twocolumn critic's best hit:** mirror symmetry is page furniture's native habit, and every surface in this house that reads as an object (the bottle, the King Wen square) has a front, a side and an off-centre feature. The seam here is off-centre — a wide plate, a narrower sheet — and the two halves are different materials at different angles.

**The only local light is the meniscus.** Nothing else moves at plane A. Nothing idles anywhere.

**Motion law, grafted from Proposal 3 (machine) and Proposal 6 (minimal):** linear or sharp-out only, no bounce, no overshoot, no easing to tune across scrub speeds. States are binary — struck or not struck. Every value is a pure function of scroll position, never a time-keyed stagger, so reversing mid-beat unwrites cleanly. One gleam is the entire spectacle budget before Beat 4; the fold is the whole spectacle budget at it.

## THE SCROLL BUDGET

| Beat | Share | The edit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — THE GIVEN | **10%** | A premise. Long enough to seat three planes and one light with nothing running. |
| 2+3 — FILL AND LETTER, one band | **68%** | Budgeted as **one line**, not two, because they share one frame and one scalar; splitting them in the budget is how "at the same time" degrades into "in sequence." Six sub-cycles of ~11.3%: working lands (≈5%), passage settles (≈4%), rest (≈2.3%). The rest is load-bearing — it is where the reader reads. |
| — THE HOLD | **6%** | All six workings visible, nothing in motion. Checkability requires a still frame; a reader adding the digits must not be punished for stopping. Cheapest 6% on the page and the stance dies without it. |
| 4 — TRAVEL AND FOLD | **16%** | One continuous movement that must read as material, not as a transition. Short enough that it never reads as loading. |

Total 100%. At ~6 screen-heights that is **~0.68 screens per mark — roughly double the current third-of-a-screen rate.** That is the edit, and it is deliberate: a line of working plus a passage of prose cannot be read in a third of a screen.

---

## WHAT IT IS NOT

- **Not a worksheet.** One line per mark, dim and small; three arithmetic, three draw-notation. If a seventh line appears anywhere, the surface has become a spec sheet.
- **Not correspondence.** No "Dear reader," no signature, no handwriting face. That is the costume nearest the prior failure.
- **Not blurred.** Text never resolves through defocus. The darkroom proposal's own §5 convicts it: blurred arithmetic reads as broken rendering, and the one content that must stay legible throughout is exactly the content it would break.
- **Not a mirror.** No 50/50 two-pane with a centre divider. That skeleton is a diff viewer.
- **Not parallax.** One scroll rate. Three independently offset layers jank on a real trackpad and buy nothing the occlusion does not already prove.
- **Not time-keyed.** No `180ms` ramps, no `90ms` staggers. Position in, value out.
- **Not ambient.** Nothing pulses, drifts, loops or breathes at rest.

---

## STILL THE BUILDER'S TO SETTLE

1. **The two values only an eye can set:** how dim the working may be and still be checkable, and how strong the single gleam is. The "i cant see" standard was learned on this very surface.
2. **Whether 0.68 screens per mark gears or drags.** The edit is argued; it has never been felt.
3. **Whether the sheet's 0.4° off-axis reads as deliberate or as a bug.** It is the design's main anti-symmetry move and the one most likely to read as a mistake.
4. **The six passages, read aloud before any motion is built.** If they do not survive being spoken, the plate is the hero and the sheet gets shorter.

**One build blocker, flagged:** `FIXTURE_SPECIFICATION.md` verifies **Margaret, 15 April 1988** — the John Bon six have never been engine-generated. Leo, Snake and 9 are derivable by hand; the rune, trigram and hexagram must come out of `birthReading()` with the verification test attached, never typed. A tutorial that teaches a wrong derivation is the one failure this design cannot absorb.

# ★ VERIFIED AGAINST THE REPO — two corrections, and the blocker restated

*Added by the main loop after the fleet returned. The body above is left exactly as the
synthesiser wrote it, so these read as corrections rather than as a quiet edit.*

## 1. `docs/FIXTURE_SPECIFICATION.md` does not exist

The blocker at the foot of the document cites a file that is not in this repository, and
attributes to it a verification of "Margaret, 15 April 1988". No such file, and no such
fixture. This is the fleet inventing a source — the exact failure mode
`docs/CLAIM_AUDIT_V1.md` was written for, arriving inside a document that argues for
checkability. Do not go looking for it.

## 2. The example rune is wrong

The body illustrates the draw notation with `→ URUZ`. The shipped fixture's rune is
**Raidho**. Every worked example in a future build must be read out of the fixture, never
composed to illustrate a point.

## 3. THE BLOCKER IS REAL, and it is worse than the fleet stated

The concern underneath the invented citation holds, and checking it found something
sharper. The six marks are **hardcoded string literals** in `_u1-rack.js:178-185` — a
`SIX` array of slot, name, provenance and keywords — under a comment claiming they are
"Derived against the shipped engine". They are not derived at runtime. They are typed.

That is not hypothetical drift. It has already fired once, and it is recorded a dozen
lines above the array itself: at **BR-S500**, four of the six rows were found to be
**inventing the codex's own keywords** — Snake printed "restraint", which is not Snake's
at all but Hexagram 7's, sitting two rows below on the same card. Four of six wrong, on
the one surface whose entire argument is that these marks are looked up rather than made
up, under a comment warning that this must never happen.

★ **So the consequence for this concept is specific and it is load-bearing.** This design
puts the *working* on screen — `2+6+0+7+1+9+6+5 = 36 → 3+6 = 9`, and a draw notation for
the other three. Hardcoding a keyword produced four wrong rows. Hardcoding **arithmetic**
produces a tutorial that teaches a false derivation, which `U1_MOTION_V1` names as the one
failure this design cannot absorb. The rule follows without argument:

> Every number and every mark shown on U1 must come out of the reading engine at build or
> run time, with its verification attached. Not one typed digit.

This is a genuine precondition on Beat 2, and it is the first thing to build — before any
plate, any sheet, any motion.

## What the main loop is NOT signing off

The material section describes a plate, a sheet at 0.4° off-axis, a meniscus crossing two
surfaces, and a fold. That is a coherent intent and it is well argued. It is also entirely
unseen — nothing in it has been rendered, and the fleet that wrote it could not have
judged it if it had been. `U1_MOTION_V1`'s own closing law applies unchanged:

> **The eye goes in the loop, or the surface does not get built. Objects first, mechanism
> second.**

The next step for this document is not implementation. It is one still frame — the plate,
the sheet, one filled slot, one passage, at rest — put beside the codex and the card, and
judged. If it is the weakest thing on that shelf, this document is a good idea that has
not earned a build yet.
