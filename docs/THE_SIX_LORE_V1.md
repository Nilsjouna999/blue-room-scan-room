# THE SIX — where each mark comes from

**Filed 2026-08-14.** The builder asked for "a bit about the origins of each read, or telling
about them in an interesting way — the lore of each of the readings."

★ **It already exists, and it is ours.** Every bank in `codex-data.json` carries a
`systemNote` written for the Codex. Any origins copy on the reading page must be condensed
from these rather than written from general knowledge — otherwise the page and the archive
say different things about the same system, which is the one contradiction a page selling

## THE SIX THAT A BIRTH READING USES

**SUN SIGN — Western Zodiac · 12 in the archive**
A birth chart puts three inner-life pillars in a sign and they do not repeat each other.
The Sun sign is **core identity** — ego, will, the basic self one is growing into. It is
what a person means when they say *my sign*. (The Moon sign is the inner and emotional
nature; the Rising is the face turned outward. This reading takes the Sun.)

**YEAR ANIMAL — Chinese Zodiac · 12 animals, 17 in the archive**
One of twelve animals to each lunar year, on a fixed twelve-year cycle, layered with one of
five elements that turn through a **sixty-year stem-branch combination**. The animal is the
**public, outward self**; the element is the material it was forged from and colours how
those traits come out. ★ The year turns at Chinese New Year in late January or February,
not on 1 January — a real edge case the reading has to handle.

**LIFE PATH — Numerology · 9 in the archive**
The same core number is read in four positions of a chart, each answering a different
question. The Life Path is calculated **from the full birth date** and describes the
overall purpose and **the main lesson of a lifetime**. (Expression comes from the name,
not the date — which is why this reading uses the Life Path and not that one.)

**RUNE — Norse, Elder Futhark · 24 in the archive**
Twenty-four staves, drawn from a pouch or cast onto a cloth, read singly or in a small
spread. Most can land upright or reversed, which darkens, blocks or delays the upright
sense — but a handful (Gebo, Hagalaz, Isa, Jera, Ingwaz, Dagaz, sometimes Sowilo) are
symmetrical and are read the same either way. ★ **A stave that cannot be reversed** is a
genuinely interesting fact and it is in our own archive.

**TRIGRAM — The Eight Trigrams (bagua) · 8 in the archive**
The three-line building blocks of the I Ching. Each carries a **natural image**, a **family
role** — father, mother, three sons and three daughters, ranked by which line differs from
the parent — and a core attribute that colours any hexagram it appears in.

**HEXAGRAM — The I Ching · 64 in the archive**
The sixty-four figures of the **King Wen sequence**, in the Wilhelm–Baynes names. Each is
**two trigrams stacked**, an inner over an outer. So the trigram and the hexagram in this
reading are not two unrelated marks — one is built out of the other, which is the single
most interesting relationship among the six.

## WHAT MAKES THIS WORTH WRITING, rather than a list of facts

★ **The six are not six versions of the same idea.** They answer different questions from
different places and different centuries: identity (Greek and Babylonian sky), the outward
self (a sixty-year Chinese cycle), the lesson of a lifetime (a number from the date), a
cast stave (Germanic), and two Chinese figures where one is literally assembled from the
other. That is the argument for reading them together, and it is a fact rather than a claim.

★ **This is what makes the SHELF angle concrete.** "One place instead of a thousand
websites" is abstract until you can say: *these are five traditions that never met, and the
last two are stacked out of the same three-line pieces.*

★ **The trigram-inside-the-hexagram relationship is the detail to lead with** if only one
piece of lore can be told. It is true, checkable in our own Codex, surprising to almost
everyone, and it demonstrates that the six were chosen rather than collected.

★ **What NOT to do:** do not explain the systems. The page's job is to make a reader want
the reading, and a paragraph teaching numerology is the curse of knowledge arriving in
person. One line of origin per mark, and a link into the Codex for anyone who wants the
rest — the archive is right there and it is free.

## COUNTS, for anyone tempted to round them

`12 + 17 + 12 + 22 + 56 + 24 + 8 + 64 + 7 = 222` across nine banks that carry entries.
A tenth (Tarot — Reading the Cards) exists as a bank with zero entries. The Lexicon's 7 are
Blue Room's own terms, not a borrowed omen system — worth knowing before anyone writes
"222 ancient marks", which would be false.
