# U1 — WHAT IT IS MEANT TO BE

*Written 2026-08-15, on request: "could you write on paper what i want u1 to be in the
best possible way."*

**This is not a design.** `docs/U1_MASTERCLASS_V1.md` is a design, and it is one fleet's
answer. This document is the thing that should outlive it: the clearest statement of what
the builder wants U1 to BE, so that any future design can be judged against something
other than taste.

**★ ITS COMPANION IS `docs/U1_MOTION_V1.md`, and that one is the more specific
authority.** This file says what U1 is FOR. That file says what it DOES — the builder's
own four-beat description of the descent: the given, the fill, the letter written in
parallel, and the travel that resolves into a record. Where the two touch, the motion
document is the later and closer statement of intent and wins.

**HOW TO READ IT.** Three registers, kept deliberately separate, because the difference
matters more than the content:

> **SAID** — the builder's own words, quoted, with where they are recorded.
> **DECIDED** — a ruling he made, with the reason he gave for it.
> **READ** — my inference. Mine, not his. Every one of these is strikeable, and if one is
> wrong the rest of the document still stands.

---

## 1. THE ONE SENTENCE

**U1 is the floor under the front door, and its single job is to answer *what is this*
for someone who has just arrived — by showing them the archive working, not by describing
it.**

Everything below either supports that sentence or narrows it.

---

## 2. WHAT HAS BEEN SAID

### On how it should move — the clearest statement of intent in the whole repo

> **SAID** *(recorded in `_u1-rack-lab.html`, the brief for THE RACK)*
>
> "as you scroll down info gets filled and you keep scrolling and it transforms into
> readings and results in boxes 2 after another keeps popping from the background. **it's
> not a separate object, is background that works like clockwork as you scroll**"
>
> "like one of those fancy new type web design when you scroll wild things happen, **only
> we have a vision and plan and we like it optimized**"

Two independent constraints live in those two sentences, and they pull against each other
on purpose:

1. **The mechanism is the page, not an object on it.** "Not a separate object" was said
   as a rejection — it is why `_u1-turn-lab.html` was refused. A widget that sits on U1
   and does something is the wrong shape. The background itself is what works.
2. **Spectacle is allowed, but only earned.** "Wild things happen" is explicitly wanted.
   "Only we have a vision and plan and we like it optimized" is the leash: the difference
   between this and the sites being referenced is that here the motion is *saying
   something*, and it costs what it should.

### On what U1 must not carry

> **SAID** *(recorded at `app.js` renderAbout, BR-S457)*
>
> "current u1 those rooms deleted, only exist at room button. and road map on button pop
> up where the button is."

### On why the roadmap is a separate destination

> **SAID** *(recorded in the roadmap decision, 2026-08-11)*
>
> "It's more hype when someone finds it in rooms page and starts checking what's coming."

### On the state it is in now

> **SAID** *(this session)* — "if the current u1 was worst possible result of the concept
> and idea, and they have to redesign in to masterclass page"

That is a judgement on the execution, not on the idea. The concept is not in question.

### On tuning, and the standard

> **SAID** *(BR-S258, on the stencil at alpha .092)* — "i cant see"

Three words that set a standard the whole project now runs on: a number derived from an
argument is worth nothing until an eye has found it on a dark screen. It is quoted here
because U1 is where it was learned.

## 3. WHAT IS DECIDED — and must not be re-proposed

**These are settled. A design that reopens one has misread the brief, not disagreed with it.**

| Decided | The reason given |
|---|---|
| **The room list leaves U1.** | The rooms have a ROOMS button that opens a map of them. U1 was a second, longer list of the same doors, one scroll below the first. |
| **The roadmap leaves U1** and becomes its own destination, reached from the ROOMS hub. | Discovery is the feature. A roadmap you *find* is an event; a roadmap you scroll to the bottom of is a footnote. This **reverses** an earlier plan to combine them — the combo is rejected. |
| **U1 keeps ONE link out to the roadmap.** That link is its entire relationship to it. | Cohesion was argued for and overruled. |
| **U1 explains the CURRENT products**, not the coming ones. | Shipped and coming must not be merged into one spine. That was the rejected combo. |
| **Keep the Blue Room voice.** | The instinct to make it "less Blue Room tone, more understandable" was tested against cold readers and the opposite was true: nobody bounced on ornateness. Every real bounce was on a word standing in for a **fact that exists in the code and was never written on the page**. The fix is the concrete noun or the number — never a flatter register. |
| **The mechanism is the background, not an object.** | "It's not a separate object." |

## 4. WHAT U1 IS NOT

- **Not a map.** Two of its three original jobs were taken away precisely because they
  had better addresses. What is left is the one job only U1 can do.
- **Not a roadmap.** It links to one. It does not contain one.
- **Not a sales page.** The house register rules out urgency, and equally rules out
  defensiveness — a page that argues with an objection nobody raised has already lost.
- **Not a document that happens to hang under a room.** You *descend* into it. That
  descent is the strongest fact about the surface.
- **Not a place to explain the joke.** Whatever U1 shows should be legible without a
  caption telling the reader what they just saw.

---

## 5. THE REGISTER IT HAS TO HOLD

The house register, which governs every surface:

> **"Holding your excitement, in knowing reassurance it will deliver."**

For U1 specifically that resolves to: **the page should be confident enough not to
oversell, and interesting enough not to need to.** The reader should leave believing the
archive is real — because they watched it do something — not because the page told them
it was serious.

And the spatial law, which governs where things are:

> **"Things be where you think they be."**

U1 is where you go when you look *down* from the front door. Nothing in it should require
knowing that in advance.

---

## 6. THE STANDARD IT IS BEING HELD TO NOW

Three measured facts about the current page, from this session's review. They are here as
the bar, not as a complaint:

1. **The largest type on the page belongs to a fixture person who does not exist.** A
   sample name is set larger than the house's own motto.
2. **The descent lands on an empty label.** At the top of the scroll the intake reads
   "The reading is taken from" followed by nothing.
3. **It is not on the launch build at all.** The mechanism is preview-only, so the U1 a
   visitor actually gets today is a header, a footer, and a hole between them.

**A masterclass U1 is one where none of those three sentences could be written.**

---

## 7. WHAT IS STILL THE BUILDER'S CALL

Open, and nobody else should close them:

- **What U1 is called.** "About Blue Room" was judged likely wrong once the page's job
  got sharper. Unresolved.
- **Whether THE RACK is the seed of U1 or a beautiful thing in the wrong place.** It is
  the only part anyone has fought for, and it is also currently the only thing on the page
  besides two paragraphs. Both readings are live.
- **How much scroll the mechanism may spend.** The current rate is roughly a third of a
  screen per mark. Whether that reads as gearing or as the page failing to keep up is an
  eye call and has never been made.
- **Whether the frame appears on hover** (at rest a hairline and a platemark; on hover the
  photographed frame fades in). His own idea, left on a toggle, still open.

## 8. WHERE I AM INFERRING — strike any of these freely

> **READ.** The two sentences about the rack are not one instruction, they are a
> *specification and its budget*. "Wild things happen" is the ambition; "we have a vision
> and plan and we like it optimized" is the acceptance test. A U1 that is calm and cheap
> has failed the first half. A U1 that is spectacular and expensive has failed the second.
> Both halves have to pass, and that is harder than either alone — which is probably why
> this surface has been rebuilt more than any other.

> **READ.** "It's not a separate object" is the most load-bearing thing said about U1,
> and it is easy to hear as a note about a widget. I think it is a claim about *what kind
> of thing a page is*. Everything else on this site is objects on grounds — cards, plates,
> doors, seals. U1 is the one surface where the builder asked for the ground itself to be
> the content. That makes it structurally unlike every other surface in the house, and
> designs keep failing on it because they bring the object habit with them.

> **READ.** The three jobs U1 used to have were not equally weighted. Two were
> *navigation* and one was *identity*. Cutting the navigation was not a subtraction — it
> was a clarification that identity was always the job. The page has not yet been rebuilt
> around that; it was pruned and left.

> **READ.** The strongest available answer to "what is Blue Room" is not a sentence. It
> is watching six marks be derived from a name and a date, each one showing its own
> working, and ending with a name nobody chose. That is the product, performed. The reason
> to show the arithmetic — `1+9+6+5+0+7+2+6 = 36 → 3+6 = 9` — is that it converts the
> site's central claim, *given not chosen*, from something the page asserts into something
> the reader can check. This is the one inference I would defend hardest.

> **READ.** U1 probably needs a floor. Today it ends and there is nothing at the bottom —
> no exit, no next thing, nothing focusable in the whole section. A descent that arrives
> nowhere reads as an unfinished page regardless of how good the descent was.

---

## 9. THE TEST

A future U1 is right when a stranger who has scrolled it once can say, without being told:

1. **what Blue Room does** — it reads people from small given things and files what it finds;
2. **that it is real** — because they watched it derive something rather than claim it;
3. **where to go next** — one door, taken deliberately, not a list;

and cannot say:

4. that anything on the page moved for the sake of moving.
