# THE TURNING WORLD — concept brainstorm

**Status:** written 2026-08-12. The builder picked "the turning world" out of the
six and asked for the concept to be worked out before anything is built. This
document does that. It builds nothing.

Reads on from `THE_PASSENGER_FEELING_V1.md` (the feeling, its eight laws, and the
mapping from reading-marks to kingdom).

---

## 1. THE SIX QUESTIONS THE CONCEPT HAS TO ANSWER

1. What is turning?
2. Where is the viewer standing?
3. How fast, and against what clock?
4. What do you actually see go past?
5. What happens at the far side?
6. What is it made of?

Everything else is craft. Get these six wrong and no amount of craft saves it.

---

## 2. WHAT IS TURNING — five candidates

| | Form | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A small globe**, whole, seen from outside | Instantly legible; obviously a world | The bauble problem: you look AT it, so you are an observer, not a passenger |
| **B** | **A cylinder / band** scrolling sideways | Cheapest; no far side to solve | Reads as a carousel or a slot reel; no depth, no world |
| **C** | **A disc seen at an angle** — a turntable, an orrery plate | Beautiful, archival, very Blue Room | Reads as a *model of* a place rather than a place |
| **D** | **A very large sphere, only its top arc visible** | You are ON it. A horizon that curves. Continues past the frame both sides. | Hardest to make legible — you see less of it |
| **E** | **Nested rings turning at different rates** (armillary) | Gorgeous, deeply on-brand for an archive that reads charts | Abstract; hard to hold a village |

### ★ RECOMMENDATION: **D**, with a debt to **E**

**Not a globe you look at — a horizon that curves because the world is round.**

This is the one form that dissolves the bauble problem outright. A globe on a
stand makes you an observer of a specimen. A curved horizon crossing the whole
width, with the crown standing on it and things cresting over it, makes you a
passenger on the thing that is turning. It also matches the builder's own sketch
exactly: a horizon **crossing the full width** behind the column, at two depths.

The debt to **E**: whatever else moves — the sky, a further plane, weather —
should turn at a *different rate* from the ground. Two speeds is what makes it
read as a system with depth rather than one image sliding.

---

## 3. WHERE THE VIEWER STANDS

**Just above the surface, at a great distance.** Not in orbit (that is A), not
standing in the village (that is a game).

The practical consequence: **the curve should be very shallow.** A strong curve
reads as a small planet, which reads as cartoon. A shallow curve — a few degrees
of arc across 1600px — reads as *the Earth, from far enough back to see that it
bends.* Solemn, not cute. This single number is the difference between an archive
and a mobile game, and it should be one of the first things the fleet tunes.

---

## 4. THE ROTATION — and the idea that solves the hardest law

L4 says: no perceptible loop. The instant you can see the seam, the world stops
being a world. A turning thing returns. That is the concept's central problem.

### ★ THE ANSWER: ONE REVOLUTION PER DAY, ON THE REAL CLOCK

The kingdom turns once every 24 hours, locked to the visitor's actual local time.

Everything falls out of this at once:

- **The loop is 24 hours, so nobody ever sees it close.** L4 is not mitigated, it
  is dissolved.
- **The page is genuinely different at every visit**, with nobody earning, buying,
  or returning on a streak. The "tenth visit" test passes for free.
- **It turned while you were away.** That is the exact feeling asked for — the
  world kept going without you, which is what makes sitting still feel like rest
  rather than waiting.
- **Time-of-day comes free.** There is a lit side and a dark side. At 3am your
  kingdom is in the small hours; at noon it is in the light. No separate theming
  feature, no clock shown, no copy explaining it.
- **It is honest.** Blue Room reads birth charts. A page whose world turns on the
  real day is the same claim its products make, made physical.

**Speed check:** a 24h revolution moves a point on the horizon by about
0.004 degrees per second. Over a two-minute visit, roughly half a degree. You will
never catch it moving. You *will* notice, coming back at night, that the world
has turned. That is precisely the car: you do not feel the motion, you notice you
are somewhere else.

**The one problem it creates:** you cannot demo it. The fleet must build a
time-scrub (`?t=03:00`) so a whole day can be judged in ten seconds, and that
control must never ship.

## 5. WHAT GOES PAST

Ordered by how much each is worth against how hard it is:

- **The terrain itself** — your trigram's land, rotating. Mountains crest and go.
  This is the main event and it is free once the curve exists.
- **The lights** — life path count, on the dark side, off in daylight. The cheapest
  possible "somebody lives here", and it is *your number*.
- **The neighbours' lights** — one per ring, further along the curve, coming round.
  Someone you read for is over there.
- **The creature** — your year animal, somewhere on it, small, moving at its own
  pace, never arriving (L2).
- **Weather** — the hexagram, the only thing permitted to change quickly, and even
  then slowly.
- **The monuments** — minted cards, standing, catching the light as they crest.
- **The sky** — the sun sign's constellation, on the *further* plane, turning at a
  different rate (the debt to E).
- **Tarot** — whatever passes overhead tonight. The one element that is a *moment*
  rather than a structure, which is what a drawn card is.

**The rule that keeps it weather:** everything crests, crosses and leaves. Nothing
arrives, completes, or waits for you.

---

## 6. THE FAR SIDE

Three honest options:

1. **There is no far side.** Only an inhabited band is ever modelled; the rest of
   the revolution is empty land, water, or night. Cheapest, and defensible — most
   of a world is empty.
2. **The far side is the night side.** The same features, unlit. Costs nothing
   extra and doubles the apparent content.
3. **The far side is other people.** Reserved: if profiles are ever visitable, the
   far side is where the neighbours actually are.

**Recommend 1 + 2 now, and design so 3 remains possible.**


---

## 6b. ★ RINGS BRING THEIR OWN MARKS — the kingdom is a household

The builder's clarification, and it upgrades the whole idea:

> *"you can customize or change results if you have access to the result — aka
> mother got horse and you got tiger — on ring."*

A **ring** is earned by reading for someone close. That reading produces **their
six marks**, and you hold the result. So a ring does not add a generic neighbour's
light. It adds **that person, as themselves**:

```
   you            Tiger,  on your trigram's land
   your mother    Horse,  on HERS
```

Her animal. Her terrain. Her sky. Standing in your world because you read for her.

### WHY THIS IS THE STRONGEST PIECE OF THE SYSTEM

- **The kingdom stops being a self-portrait and becomes a household.** That is a
  far better thing to sit in front of, and it is the difference between a trophy
  case and a place where people live.
- **The ring finally has an object.** It is currently a row on the shelf that says
  "none set yet" — the emptiest line on the page. Now a ring is *a horse on a hill
  that was not there last month*.
- **It gives the paid act a visible consequence.** Reading for someone is the one
  product that is about another person; this is the only mechanic in the building
  that shows it.
- **It cannot be faked or farmed.** You cannot place a horse. You can only read for
  someone who has a birthday, and then it is there.

### ARRANGEMENT IS THE CUSTOMISATION — earned, and safe

*"you can customize or change results if you have access to the result."*

Because you hold the result, you may **arrange** it: where on the sphere that
person's land sits, how near your own, which face of the world they share. That
is the decorating mechanic the dwelling research argued for — and it is the safe
form of it, because:

- **you can only arrange what you have earned.** Nothing is bought, nothing is
  invented, and nobody can place a thing they did not read for.
- **every piece is generated from real marks**, so no arrangement can produce
  something off-brand. The aesthetic cannot be wrecked by a user, which was the
  research's central worry about customisation.
- **it is placement, not styling.** No colour pickers, no fonts, no stickers. You
  are moving land, and the land already looks like Blue Room.

This is "decorate within the system" (`PROFILE_REDESIGN_BRIEF_V1` §9c principle 4)
in its strictest and best form.

### ★ AND IT IS WHY THE 24-HOUR ROTATION IS THE RIGHT CHOICE

With several rings, the world holds more than the visible arc can show. The
rotation decides **who is on your horizon right now**.

> Your mother's horse comes round in the evening.

That is not a feature anybody has to explain. It means:

- **the world is bigger than the screen**, permanently — the strongest possible
  answer to "empty"
- **returning at a different hour shows you different people**, which gives a
  reason to open the page at night that is memory and rhythm rather than a streak
- **more rings never crowds the view.** A twelfth ring does not squeeze the others;
  it takes its own place on the sphere and waits its turn. The layout problem that
  kills most "your collection, visualised" features does not arise.
- and it means the arrangement decision above is a real one: placing someone near
  you means you see them together; placing them opposite means you see them at
  opposite ends of the day.

### WHAT THIS ADDS TO THE OPEN QUESTIONS

7. **Does a ring bring one mark or all six?** All six is a whole second kingdom
   and will crowd fast. Recommend: **their creature and their terrain**, and the
   rest of their marks stay in the reading itself, on the shelf.
8. **Can a ring's land be adjacent to yours, or must it be its own island?**
   Adjacency says family; islands say distinct people. Probably a choice, and
   probably the most meaningful arrangement decision on offer.
9. **What happens to a ring you regret?** There must be a way to remove someone
   from your world that is quiet, immediate and unpunished.
10. **Does the Concord (two people, one reading) produce one shared land or two?**

## 7. WHAT IT IS MADE OF

**SVG, engraved line, gold on near-black.** Not canvas, not WebGL, not raster.

Reasons, in order:

- Blue Room's entire visual language is already engraved line — the crown, the
  emblems, the codex marks, the seals. A turning SVG world is the same hand.
- The references are all line: an old globe, an armillary sphere, a map's
  hatching, an orrery's brass.
- It is crisp at every size and cheap to composite (L6): a rotating `<g>` and a
  clip, transform-only, no per-frame layout.
- It survives reduced motion honestly (L7) — stop the transform and you have an
  engraved map of your kingdom, which is still a place, and arguably a nicer one.
- 10,368 combinations means the parts must be *composable*. Line art composes;
  painted art does not.

**The hard craft problem, stated plainly:** a Mountain and a Lake must be
distinguishable at 40px, in one colour, at a glance, with no label. That is a
silhouette problem, and it is the whole job. Eight terrains, twelve creatures. If
the fleet solves nothing else, it must solve this.

## 8. HOW IT MEETS THE EIGHT LAWS

| Law | How |
|---|---|
| L1 passenger, not driver | Nothing responds to the cursor. The clock drives it, and the clock does not know you are there. |
| L2 weather, not events | Everything crests and leaves. Nothing arrives. |
| L3 behind, not on | The world is below and behind the frame; the sky is further still, turning slower. |
| L4 no perceptible loop | **Solved outright** — the loop is 24 real hours. |
| L5 content always wins | It sits above the line only. Nothing crosses downward. Low contrast, no motion fast enough to catch the eye. |
| L6 costs nothing | One transform on one `<g>`, driven by a clock read once a minute — not a rAF loop. |
| L7 reduced motion | Stop the transform. An engraved map remains. |
| L8 indifferent, not lonely | Lights are on somewhere. Something crested a while ago. |

★ Note L6: because the rotation is 0.004°/s, this **does not need a per-frame
animation at all**. Set the transform from the clock once a minute and let CSS
interpolate, or simply re-set it. This is close to free — cheaper than the
membrane effect that was just retired.

## 9. THE OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE FLEET

1. **How shallow is the curve?** The single number that decides archive vs cartoon.
2. **How much of the sphere is visible** — a sliver, a third, half?
3. **Does the crown stand ON the horizon, or above it?** Standing on it makes the
   kingdom the crown's; floating above keeps the crown a monument.
4. **Where exactly is the line?** Is the threshold the horizon itself, or is the
   horizon above the threshold with sky between?
5. **Do the sides blur, or does the world simply get darker toward the edges?**
   Blur is expensive and soft; a vignette is cheap and holds the line quality.
6. **What does an empty kingdom look like** — a visitor with only a free tarot
   pull, so a sky and no land yet?

---

## 10. WHAT THE FLEET BUILDS

Not six different worlds any more — **six attempts at THIS world**, differing on
questions 1-6 above. Each delivers:

- a standalone page, the real profile content in the frame, the turning world above
  the line
- the seed **Mountain · Snake · Aries · Life Path 7 · Gebo**
- one alternate seed (**Lake · Horse · Pisces · Life Path 3 · Ansuz**) to prove it
  composes
- a `?t=` time-scrub so a full day is judgeable in seconds
- the reduced-motion still
- one line on which of the eight laws it found hardest to hold

The builder judges by sitting in front of it for sixty seconds and asking whether
the stillness feels like rest or like waiting.
