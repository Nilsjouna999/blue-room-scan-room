## The protocol, as received

### 1. The diagnosis
The page has a **contrast-pair engine**: nearly every idea arrives escorted by the
thing it is better than. *Rooms rather than features. Rest rather than idleness. A
place, not another item in a list. As marks, never as a feed.* Individually
defensible; accumulated, the page sounds like it is in a long-running argument with
an industry the reader may never have thought about — and slightly proud of winning.

Two edges: **refusal** (says what it isn't more than what it is) and **superiority**
(compliments itself on its own restraint). The cure for both is **letting claims
stand alone**.

### 2. What should compound
> Something enters the archive. The archive keeps it. What is kept gathers into a
> place of your own. What gathers becomes a world. That world can hold other people
> without becoming a feed. The maker promises to keep its character. You are invited
> to change it. Support is welcome and never asked for.

Each passage should TRANSFORM the one before, not restate the ethics. Ownership and
non-coercion currently appear in *The Archive*, *Further On* AND *Leave Something*.
The recurring nouns — enter, keep, gather, place, room, door, return, mark — are the
real cohesive device.

### 3. The waves
| Wave | The ask | Voice | Fails if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threshold | Look up | Spare, strange, short | It explains |
| The Archive | Understand the premise | Warm, concrete, unhurried | It defends |
| Open Now | Believe it exists | Plain, factual, unpoetic | It gets lyrical |
| Further On | Imagine forward | Visual, structural, light | It promises dates |
| The Covenant | Trust the maker | Firm, accountable, once | It compares itself |
| Send Something Back | Contribute | Practical, courteous | It gets clever |
| Leave Something | Decide freely | Gentle, unembarrassed | It moralises |
| Close | Leave | Very plain | It chimes |

**The failure is in the last third.** The page ends: manifesto refusal → correction
form → contribution rules → "we don't want it." The reader's final experience is
being turned down. It should crest at the covenant then DESCEND, and end on USE.

### 4. Section notes (abridged to the operative calls)
- **Keep unaltered:** "Blue Room is an archive that reads things." · "What costs
  money says so on its own door, once, in plain figures." · "It is made of rooms
  rather than features." · "Every way in is a way back out." · the trigram/land
  passage · "Other people arrive as marks, never as a feed." · the love line.
- **"Nothing here is built to make you come back"** posits an adversary and
  contradicts what an archive is. Try: *An archive is worth returning to on its own.
  Nothing here will chase you.*
- **Cut "apologise for them"**, keep the rest of that sentence.
- **Split the object dump** (Shelf, crown, gem, mark, ring in one breath) into
  naming, then accumulating.
- **Cut the comparison entirely:** "Blue Room is not trying to look like anything
  else and is not going to get louder to be noticed."
- **Pick a person** — Blue Room for the place, I for promises, you for invitations.
  Kill every stray *we*. "I" makes promises credible but makes the world smaller;
  confine it to the covenant so the contrast is itself an effect.
- **Break the four-beat:** *No counters. No streaks. Nothing here is keeping score
  of you.*
- **Remove before launch:** any explanation that the form isn't connected yet.
- **End on use:** *Only if it's spare. Otherwise just use the archive — that's what
  it's for.*

### 5. Layout as rhythm
Measure in `ch`, not `rem`. Manifesto column **62–72ch**; narrow is part of the
argument. Density varies by wave: *Open Now* tightest, *Further On* airiest, and
**the page physically opens at the form (~80–86ch) then closes again to say
goodbye.** Gold stays scarce: section labels, the selected response type, the
closing refrain.

### 6. Not sounding like a machine
1. The contrast-pair engine — **cut two thirds**. Test: delete the rejected
   alternative; if the sentence still stands it was stronger alone.
2. **Tricolons** — the most reliable LLM fingerprint. Keep the best two.
3. **Every section ending on an aphorism** — let at least three end ordinarily.
4. **Uniform sentence length** — humans lurch. Put a four-word sentence beside a
   forty-word one.
5. **Em-dash appositives** — ration them.
6. **No grit** — nothing that could only have come from this person. Add two or
   three burrs: a real date, a real number, one ordinary word where a beautiful one
   would fit, one sentence that's slightly blunt.

Reference sample for the whole voice, already on the page: *"Your trigram is the
land. Your year animal walks on it. Your sun sign is the sky over it, and your rune
stands in it as a stone."* Four declaratives. No contrast pairs, no negation, no
em-dashes.

### 7. The tonal rule
**Positive language builds the world. Negative language defends the READER — never
defines Blue Room against anyone else.** Firm exactly once, at the covenant. Keep
firmness at: the price-disclosure line, "never as a feed", and the score-keeping
promise.

### 8. The question the audit didn't ask
**Should this page be this long?** Cut it to half and see what dies. If nothing
essential does, the compounding was thinner than it looked.

### 9. Edit protocol — run in this order
1. Delete every sentence that positions Blue Room against something else.
2. Find each promise stated twice; keep the strongest.
3. Cut two thirds of the contrast pairs.
4. Fix the ending — last impression must be use, not refusal.
5. Choose the person and enforce it.
6. Break the rhythm — count tricolons and aphoristic closers.
7. Add grit.
8. Only then tune spacing and measure.

**How you'll know it's working:** read it aloud to someone who has never heard of
tarot, and at no point should they wonder who you're arguing with.
