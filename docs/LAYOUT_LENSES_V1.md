# THE LENSES — how to run a layout-design fleet so its output can actually be used

**Filed 2026-08-14, from the builder's brief:**

> "later we should have fleet to put in paper different possibilities and reasoning…
> what is selling, what is visually, what is navigationally, what is in smoothness,
> emotionally, continuity, first time, understandable in many ways, how distinguish or
> and plan and implement this information properly and accurately and correctly and what
> other lenses are important for layout designing fleets"

Not to be fired yet. This is the design OF that fleet, written while the evidence for it
is still fresh.

---

## ★ THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES, learned the expensive way today

A 15-lens fleet ran on M1 and produced 91 findings. It was genuinely useful — but the
useful part came from the lenses that MEASURED something, and the rest could not be
combined with each other at all, because **every lens reported in its own currency.**
One returned line numbers, one returned adjectives, one returned a ranked list, one
returned an essay. Nothing could be laid beside anything else, so a human had to hold all
fifteen in their head to decide anything, which is precisely the work the fleet was
supposed to do.

Two rules follow, and they matter more than the lens list itself:

1. **EVERY LENS RETURNS THE SAME SHAPE.** A lens that returns prose has not done its job.
2. **THE INTERESTING INFORMATION IS IN THE CONFLICTS, NOT THE REPORTS.** Selling wants
   the price visible; restraint wants it withheld. Smoothness wants motion; first-time
   clarity wants stillness. A fleet that hides those tensions inside fifteen separate
   documents has destroyed its own most valuable output.

---

## ★★ TWO RULES THAT COME BEFORE THE LENSES

Written after the first draft of this document made SMALL SCREEN a veto. The builder:

> "somethings that does not automatically kill ideas because 1 lens disagrees, something
> like small screen, but in actually can have different designs phone and pc"

That is correct and the first draft was wrong. A design can be excellent on a wide screen
and have a *different and also excellent* answer on a phone. Demanding that one
arrangement survive every width is not rigour — it is a hidden design decision smuggled in
as a standard, and it quietly eliminates every idea whose whole strength is being suited
to one context.

### RULE 1 — THE SPLIT CLAUSE

**A candidate may declare that a context gets its own design, and each design is then
judged on its own terms.** The lens stops asking *"does this survive here"* and asks
*"is there a good answer here, and is it the same idea."*

Declarable contexts: **wide · phone · tablet · touch vs pointer · first visit vs
returning · has-holdings vs not · reduced-motion · no-JS.**

What a split must state to be legitimate, and this is the whole discipline:

1. **The idea is the same; the arrangement differs.** Two designs, one thesis. If the
   phone version argues something different from the wide one, that is not a split — it
   is two products wearing one name, and the visitor is the one who pays for it.
2. **Nothing is lost, only re-placed.** A split that deletes the offer, the price, an exit
   or a product on one context is not a split. It is a cut, and it gets judged as a cut.
3. **The seam is named.** At what width or state does it change, and what does a person
   crossing that seam experience? An undeclared seam is a bug that has not happened yet.
4. **The cost is admitted.** Two designs are two things to maintain and two things to keep
   truthful. COST and TRUTH both get to price that.

★ Consequence for the lens: **CONTEXT FIT replaces SMALL SCREEN.** It does not ask whether
the wide layout collapses well. It asks whether every declared context has a good answer,
whether the seams are honest, and whether one idea survives across all of them.

### RULE 2 — NO SINGLE LENS KILLS

Every lens returns one of four verdicts, and only the first is fatal:

| Verdict | Meaning | Who can issue it |
|---|---|---|
| **BLOCK** | This design cannot ship in any form until it is fixed. | Only against a lens's *published* kill-condition, and only when a SECOND lens independently agrees. |
| **PENALTY** | Scores badly here. Counts against it; kills nothing. | Any lens. |
| **TRADE** | Loses here *on purpose*, and the design declared it. Not a mark against it at all. | Any lens, when the candidate's declared worst-at names this lens. |
| **PASS** | No objection. | Any lens. |

Three consequences worth stating plainly:

- **A BLOCK needs a second.** One lens saying "unacceptable" is an opinion with a strong
  adjective. Two independent lenses reaching it from different directions is a finding.
  The builder's eye counts as a second, and outranks both.
- **A declared weakness is not a defect.** If a design has named the lens it accepts
  losing on, that lens returns TRADE, not PENALTY. Punishing a design for the thing it
  told you about in advance destroys the honesty the declaration was meant to buy.
- **True kill-conditions are few and must be published before judging.** The defensible
  ones: content unreachable in a declared context · a product cannot be entered ·
  the page states something untrue about the product · a control cannot be operated by
  keyboard · nothing renders without script. Everything else is a penalty.

## THE LENS SET

The builder's eight, plus the ones today's work proved were missing. Each is written as
the QUESTION it asks, because a lens named as a noun drifts into a mood.

### The builder's eight

| # | Lens | The question it asks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SELLING** | Does a visitor understand what is for sale, what it costs, and why it is worth it — and does the surface look like somewhere money is safe? |
| 2 | **VISUAL** | Composition, weight, balance, rhythm. Does the eye land where it should, in what order, and is anything winning that should not? |
| 3 | **NAVIGATIONAL** | Can you tell where you are, where you can go, and how to get back — without being told? |
| 4 | **SMOOTHNESS** | Does everything that moves feel intentional and interruptible? Does anything stutter, fight the input, or make you wait? |
| 5 | **EMOTIONAL** | What does this feel like to be in for two minutes? Not "is it pretty" — what state does it put a person in? |
| 6 | **CONTINUITY** | Does this surface belong to the same building as the others? Same grammar, same materials, same rules? |
| 7 | **FIRST TIME** | Five seconds, no context: what is this place, what is offered, what do I do? |
| 8 | **UNDERSTANDABLE MANY WAYS** | Does it survive being read differently — skimmed, read aloud, read by someone who does not know the vocabulary, read out of order? |

### The eight that were missing, and why each earned its place

| # | Lens | The question | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | **RETURNING** | Is anything faster the second time? What does someone who already knows what they want have to re-read? | Every fleet optimised the stranger. Nobody designed for the person who comes back, who is the one who pays. |
| 10 | **CONTEXT FIT** | Does every declared context have a GOOD answer — not the same answer? Are the seams named and honest? Does one idea survive all of them? | Replaces the naive "small screen" veto. Most visitors arrive on a phone, and the phone deserves a design rather than a squeeze. See the Split Clause. |
| 11 | **DEGRADED** | No JS, slow fonts, empty state, hostile content, forced colours, reduced motion. What renders? | Seven designs were beautiful and three had no first paint without script. |
| 12 | **ACCESS** | Keyboard path, focus visibility, announcement order, hit sizes, colour-only signalling. | Repeatedly found the revenue-bearing controls were the least engineered objects on the page. |
| 13 | **TRUTH** | Does the surface's claim match what the product actually does? | The strongest single idea in the competition was quoting *verbatim engine output* instead of describing it. A page that shows the goods cannot lie about them. |
| 14 | **ATTENTION** | How many things compete at the top of the luminance and motion range at once, and what does that cost? | The measurable version of "everything is screaming". Feature congestion, edge density, count of simultaneous foci. |
| 15 | **GROWTH** | What does this look like with ten more rooms, three more products, a name twice as long? | The spatial law's own test. Most designs cap silently and nobody notices until the cap is hit. |
| 16 | **SILENCE** | What is deliberately absent, and does the absence read as intent or as unfinished? | This project's signature move is withholding — and the line between *reserved* and *abandoned* is thin, learnable, and was crossed twice today. |
| 17 | **COST** | What would it take to build and to keep alive? What breaks when the code around it changes? | A design nobody can maintain is a bad design, however good the picture is. |

### The six that complete the set

| # | Lens | The question | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | **BRIEF FIT** | Is this the right surface for this content at all? Is the question we are answering the question we should be asking? | ★ The most expensive failure of the M1 work. 273 agent-runs produced seven excellent *product pages* because the brief said "sell both readings" and nobody was assigned to doubt the brief. No other lens can catch this — they all assume the target is correct. |
| 19 | **TRUST** | Does this look like it will still exist in a year, and like money is safe here? | Distinct from SELLING. Selling asks whether the offer is clear; trust asks whether a stranger believes the offer will be honoured. |
| 20 | **MEMORY & STATE** | What does this surface remember, what does it do when it forgets, and what does it claim about permanence? | "Everything read here is yours to keep" is currently one browser's localStorage. A surface that leans on state it cannot keep is writing a cheque the system does not cover. |
| 21 | **LANGUAGE** | Do the words mean what they say to someone outside this project? | This build names things beautifully and some of the names are opaque on first contact. A name that needs the house style explained is a door that needs a guide. |
| 22 | **OWNERSHIP** | Does the visitor come away with something that is theirs, and is it visible? | The project's own doctrine. It is the difference between having used a service and having been given an object, and no other lens asks it. |
| 23 | **PERFORMANCE** | What does it cost to paint, on the surface the build already calls its most paint-bound? | Promoted from optional the moment a design put a live field on M1. It is measurable, so it settles rather than argues. |

**Optional, task-dependent:** PACING (the tempo across a whole visit — where a person
slows down and speeds up), SOUND, INTERNATIONALISATION.

## ★ THE RETURN SHAPE — identical for every lens, no exceptions

```
LENS
VERDICT        one line. What this lens concludes.
RANKING        every candidate ordered on THIS lens alone, best to worst.
BASIS          the measurement, file:line, or quoted string the ranking rests on.
               A ranking with no basis is an opinion and is discarded at synthesis.
MECHANISM      what the leader actually did — the rule, the value, the structure.
               This is the line the next builder acts on.
TRADE-OFF      what winning on this lens costs on a NAMED other lens.
KILL           the condition under which a design fails this lens outright,
               regardless of how it scores elsewhere.
FREE GROUND    what NO candidate solved on this lens.
```

Two of these do the real work. **TRADE-OFF** forces every lens to admit it is not free,
which is what makes the set combinable. **FREE GROUND** is where the next round's cheapest
win lives, and it is the line that gets acted on most often.

## HOW THE INFORMATION IS COMBINED — the part that usually fails

**1. The board.** One table: candidates down the side, lenses across the top, rank in each
cell. Nothing else. It fits on a screen and it is the only artefact most people need.

**2. The conflict matrix.** Every pair of lenses whose TRADE-OFF lines name each other.
These are the real design decisions; everything else is craft. Expect the recurring ones:

- SELLING ↔ SILENCE — the price and the restraint
- SMOOTHNESS ↔ FIRST TIME — motion is atmosphere to a returning visitor and noise to a stranger
- VISUAL ↔ SMALL SCREEN — most compositions are a wide-screen argument
- EMOTIONAL ↔ TRUTH — mood wants suggestion, truth wants the actual words
- GROWTH ↔ ATTENTION — every room added is another thing competing
- ACCESS ↔ VISUAL — the quietest thing on screen is usually the one someone cannot see

**3. The forced declaration.** ★ Every candidate must name the ONE lens it accepts losing
on. This worked today and it is the single cheapest way to stop hedged designs: a design
that refuses to be worst at anything is a design with no position, and it loses to a
committed one every time.

**4. The synthesiser reads the board and the conflicts — never the reports.** If it has to
read fifteen essays to decide, the lenses failed and the shape must be fixed rather than
the synthesis worked harder.

## FLEET SHAPE

```
MEASURE   one pass, shared, across ALL candidates — not one per candidate.
          Running the same comparison per team produces N slightly different
          accounts of the same facts and teams then argue from different numbers.
          Judgement stays per-candidate; facts do not.
LENSES    one agent per lens, cheap model where the lens is countable
          (small screen, access, degraded, attention, cost), stronger model where
          it needs judgement (emotional, selling, silence, truth, first time).
REFUTE    one adversary whose only job is to strike findings that are wrong,
          restated, contradictory, or taste dressed as evidence. Default to
          rejection. Today this removed roughly a third of the findings.
CONFLICT  one agent that builds the matrix and does nothing else.
SYNTH     one agent, reads board + conflicts + free ground. Never the essays.
```

## ★★ STRESS TEST — how this framework fails, and what a bad lens run produces

Written against itself, because a rubric nobody has attacked is a rubric that will be
gamed by the first fleet that reads it. Each entry: the symptom you would actually see,
the mechanism underneath, and the guard.

### 1. THE RUBRIC-SHAPED DESIGN
**Symptom:** every candidate comes back competent, similar, and boring, and the board is
full of 2s and 3s with no 1s or 7s.
**Mechanism:** teams can read the lenses, so they design *to be scored* rather than to be
right. A published rubric is a specification, and specifications produce compliance.
**Guard:** the declared worst-at, and the mutual-cut rule. A design that offends no lens
has taken no position, and a field of near-identical designs is evidence the rubric is
doing the designing.

### 2. THE DECOY WEAKNESS
**Symptom:** every candidate declares it is worst at something nobody cares about —
"we lose on SOUND" — and collects TRADE verdicts across the board.
**Mechanism:** TRADE was invented to reward honesty, so it will be farmed the moment
honesty is optional.
**Guard:** ★ **a declared worst-at only counts if it names one of the five highest-leverage
lenses for that task.** Declaring a weakness outside the top five is not a declaration, it
is a dodge, and the lens returns PENALTY as normal.

### 3. THE SPLIT THAT NEVER GOT BUILT
**Symptom:** "the phone has its own design" appears in every sheet and no phone design
exists in any file.
**Mechanism:** the Split Clause is generous, and generosity without evidence is a licence.
**Guard:** ★ **a declared split must be BUILT to be judged.** An undesigned context is
scored as absent, not as deferred. CONTEXT FIT judges files, never intentions.

### 4. THE AVERAGE WINS
**Symptom:** the synthesis picks the design with the best mean rank, and it is the one
nobody was excited by.
**Mechanism:** 23 numbers invite a mean, and the mean of a lens board always favours the
hedge — a design that is 3rd on everything beats one that is 1st on six lenses and 7th on
two, even though the second is the only one with a reason to exist.
**Guard:** ★ **the board is never scored by average.** The synthesis reads the conflicts,
the declared positions and the free ground. If anyone computes a total, the run is void.

### 5. FALSE PRECISION BEATS IMPORTANCE
**Symptom:** paint milliseconds and contrast ratios dominate the write-up; SILENCE,
EMOTIONAL and BRIEF FIT appear as short paragraphs at the end.
**Mechanism:** a lens with a number wins any written argument against a lens without one,
regardless of which matters more. Measurability is not importance, but it reads like it.
**Guard:** the top-five weighting is set BEFORE the run and is not adjustable by evidence
quality. A measured finding on a low-leverage lens does not outrank an unmeasured finding
on a high-leverage one.

### 6. THE REFUTER OVER-CORRECTS
**Symptom:** the adversary strikes a third of the findings and the survivors are all the
trivially provable ones.
**Mechanism:** "default to rejection" is the right instruction and it selects for
provability, not truth. The most important findings are often the least citable.
**Guard:** ★ struck findings go to a **STRUCK BUT UNRESOLVED** list, not the bin. Anything
struck for being unprovable rather than wrong stays visible to the builder, who is allowed
to believe things a fleet cannot prove.

### 7. THE CONSENSUS ILLUSION
**Symptom:** every lens passes every candidate and the run feels like a success.
**Mechanism:** either the brief was leading, the candidates were all built from the same
assumption, or the lenses were run by agents who had read each other.
**Guard:** unanimity is a smell, not a result. If everything passes, suspect the brief —
and note that BRIEF FIT is the one lens that can say so.

### 8. THE EYE ARRIVES TOO LATE
**Symptom:** a beautifully argued synthesis that the builder rejects in four seconds.
**Mechanism:** the synthesis had to guess at taste, because it ran before anyone with taste
had looked. Every genuine turning point in the M1 work came from the builder looking at a
built thing.
**Guard:** ★ the eye goes in **before** synthesis, not after. Show the built candidates,
take the reaction, then synthesise with it in hand.

### 9. STALE BOARD
**Symptom:** the board says team 5 leads on CONTEXT FIT; team 5's file has been rewritten
twice since.
**Mechanism:** lens output ages the moment a builder edits, and nothing in the artefact
says which version it judged.
**Guard:** every lens report carries the file's modified-time or hash. A board older than
its files is not evidence.

### 10. WRONG MODEL ON THE WRONG LENS
**Symptom:** the SILENCE report is a list of element counts; the ACCESS report is an essay.
**Mechanism:** cheap models are excellent at counting and poor at judgement; the reverse
for strong ones. Assigning by cost rather than by lens type produces exactly this
inversion.
**Guard:** countable lenses get cheap models, judgement lenses get strong ones, and the
assignment is part of the fleet design rather than a budget decision.

### 11. THE LENS THAT NEVER SAYS ANYTHING
**Symptom:** CONTINUITY returns "consistent with the rest of the build" for every
candidate, every time.
**Mechanism:** a lens with no way to fail is decoration. It costs an agent and produces a
row of passes that pad the board.
**Guard:** every lens publishes its kill-condition before the run. A lens that cannot
state what failure looks like is cut from the set for that task.

---

★ **And the rule that outranks all of this: the builder's eye settles in seconds what a
fleet argues about for an hour.** Every genuine turning point in the M1 work came from
looking at a built thing, not from an agent's reasoning. So: build the candidates first,
run the lenses on things that exist, and put the eye in the loop before the synthesis
rather than after it.
