# BLUE ROOM — THE BIRTH PRESERVE
## Final Design Master Sheet

**Status:** Final direction — ready for visual prototyping
**Object:** A labelled preserving jar for the Birth Reading
**Context:** Additive replacement of the Birth object inside the existing Blue Room centre stage
**Governing sentence:** The six marks were given once, preserved together, and labelled without revision.
**Integration boundary:** Everything outside the existing centre-stage Birth object remains visually and behaviorally original.

## 1. Final reflection

The sticker revision is a conceptual improvement over placing a paper card inside liquid. It makes the jar itself the artifact, removes the implausible wet-paper problem, and gives the preservation metaphor a direct visual grammar: contents inside, identity outside, seal above.

It is not automatically successful. A large cream sticker can reduce an extraordinary object to premium grocery packaging, cover the very contents that make the jar recognizable, and weaken the existing card system by turning a dimensional reading object into a flat label. The design must therefore preserve three distinctions:

- Archive label, not retail label. It records and identifies; it does not advertise.
- Preserving jar, not novelty prop. The silhouette and contents must remain immediately legible.
- Birth-specific metaphor, not a skin pasted over every product. Birth can be preserved because its six marks are fixed. Tarot is drawn and should retain a different physical condition.

### Final verdict

Build this direction. The concept is now resolved enough to prototype without reopening its central decisions. Its originality does not come from making pickles luxurious; it comes from treating preservation as the physical truth of a Birth Reading. The sticker identifies what the jar has kept. The jar provides the delayed recognition. Blue Room supplies the seriousness.

The final object must be understood in two beats:

1. A carefully conserved archival reading.
2. That archival object is genuinely a pickle jar.

If the second beat arrives first, the design is too comic. If it never arrives, the design is too abstract.

### What survives

- The existing main menu outside the centre-stage Birth object, pixel-for-pixel and behavior-for-behavior.
- The existing three-column composition and all column geometry.
- The complete left product/navigation column.
- The complete right reading panel, including its styling, copy behavior and position.
- The existing Birth/Tarot selector, including its position, styling, keyboard behavior and controller.
- Global navigation, routes, hover states, focus states and responsive order.
- The real Birth Reading content and six-mark hierarchy.
- The Birth/Tarot control system and existing card-turn logic as a behavioral reference.
- A dark archival room with one warm central object.
- Pointer-reactive light rather than pointer-reactive object movement.
- The right column as the explanatory reading surface.

### What is now locked

- The Birth card becomes an exterior opaque conservation sticker adhered to the jar.
- The jar is seated inside a shallow architectural niche rather than floating on the page.
- Warmth is concentrated in the label and local brine; the cavity remains blue-black.
- Birth-mark rows become actual accessible controls.
- One recognizable pickle is the primary preserved form; all other contents remain subordinate.
- The lid carries six shallow seal notches and one narrow brass tamper strip.
- Birth and Tarot use different exhibit states rather than pretending both are preserved pickles.
- Birth uses the labelled preserve; Tarot uses a dry black-card exhibit in the same fixed stage footprint.
- No surrounding menu component is redesigned to accommodate the exhibit.
- No new customer-facing copy appears outside the centre-stage exhibit.

### What is rejected

- A full oval or capsule silhouette.
- A paper card floating inside liquid.
- A large external glow or shadow that makes the jar hover.
- Mason-jar nostalgia, farmhouse decoration, gingham, rope or rustic branding.
- Cartoon pickles, bright green brine or constant bubbling.
- Reflections crossing the opaque sticker as though it were inside the glass.
- Customer-facing development codes such as M2.
- Truncated explanatory copy.

## 2. Absolute truths

These are the non-negotiable laws of the object.

- The label is the Birth Reading; the jar is the preserving body.
- The object must read as a preserving jar in silhouette before any copy is legible.
- The label is an opaque matte conservation film permanently adhered to the exterior glass.
- The card/label remains perfectly stationary under pointer movement.
- The centre of the label is optically calm and fully readable.
- Glass refraction belongs around exposed edges, shoulders, neck and base.
- The liquid obeys gravity and has a clear horizontal meniscus.
- One recognizable pickle must remain visible beside the label; supporting contents stay peripheral.
- The card owns the warm cream; the Blue Room owns the surrounding blue-black.
- Motion occurs only as a restrained response to direct input.
- The six marks are interactive, but their printed appearance remains primary.
- Birth copy speaks of preservation, derivation and constancy—not drawing.
- Tarot copy speaks of drawing and chance—not preservation at birth.
- The design must remain understandable without hover.
- Reduced motion removes bubbles, parallax and the physical turn without removing information.
- At mobile width, the entire jar fits without horizontal clipping.
- If the vegetables become more memorable than the reading, the composition has failed.
- If the label could plausibly appear on a supermarket shelf, the taste has failed.
- The lid has six restrained notches; they are a seal system, not decoration.
- The design contains no automatic idle animation.
- Outside the current centre-stage Birth object, the original main menu is immutable.
- The jar must fit the existing stage; the menu must never be widened, shifted or simplified to fit the jar.

## 3. Taste north star

**Target:** Victorian conservation cabinet × Scandinavian preserving vessel × Blue Room archive.

The result should feel strange, dignified, precise and faintly funny. The humour comes from discovering that a solemn archival relic is genuinely a pickle jar. No line of interface copy should explain or perform the joke.

**Desired qualities:** Preserved, not refrigerated · Valuable, not luxurious-by-glow · Handmade, not distressed · Botanical, not rustic · Deadpan, not whimsical · Old-world, not steampunk · Materially credible, not photorealistic for its own sake

**Taste killers:** Bright grocery green · Red-and-white fabric patterns · Rope, raffia or kraft tags · Heavy rust, grime or torn edges · Excess condensation · Neon highlights · Busy label borders · Fake handwritten fonts · Pun-based copy · Constant liquid animation

## 4. Master visual concept

A straight-sided preserving jar sits in a shallow, blue-black wall recess. The jar is filled with restrained olive-gold brine, one recognizable preserved cucumber form, a fine dill stem, a few mustard seeds and a mineral deposit in the heavy glass foot.

A tall warm-cream archival sticker is fixed to the exterior front face. It contains the real Birth Reading hierarchy: institutional header, crowned name, six marks and closing seal. The sticker is matte and quiet; the exposed glass around it carries the reflections and material drama.

The first glance reads rare preserved object. The second glance reads pickle jar.

## 5. Shape and proportion

**Jar silhouette**
- Overall width-to-height ratio: 0.70–0.74
- Lid height: 8–10% of total jar height
- Neck: 5–7%
- Shoulder transition: 7–9%
- Straight body: 66–70%
- Heavy base/foot: 7–9%
- Corner radius: present but not capsule-like
- Centre viewing surface: subtly flattened

**Label proportion**
- Width: 55–60% of jar body width
- Height: 62–68% of jar body height
- Top position: below the shoulder, never touching the meniscus
- Bottom position: above the heavy glass foot
- Corner radius: 2–3% of label width
- Side curvature: only the outermost 5–7% bends with the jar

**Composition**
- Jar occupies approximately 42–46% of the available centre-column width on a 1440–1800px desktop composition.
- Label remains the brightest contiguous plane.
- Exposed liquid should remain visible as two vertical bands around the label and one band below it.
- The niche supplies the contact shadow; the jar receives no theatrical exterior glow.

**Niche construction**
- Shape: rectangular with nearly square corners
- Apparent depth: 18–24px
- Interior: blue-black backplate, cooler than the brine
- Edge: one thin antique-gold interior seam
- Base: shallow internal sill carrying the jar's contact shadow and caustic
- Exterior shadow: none
- Relationship to footer: visibly separate; the jar never appears supported by global navigation

## 6. Material system

**Glass:** Smoke-blue clear glass · Thick rim and foot · One faint vertical manufacturing seam · Mild shoulder waviness · Cool edge reflection on one side · Narrow neutral highlight at the shoulder · No universal white outline

**Brine:** Centre translucent champagne · Mid-field muted olive-gold · Edge density deep bottle green · Lower accumulation dark tea-green · Meniscus thin, level and unmistakable · No milkiness across the label field

**Lid:** Blackened brass or dark blue enamel · Fine radial or vertical grooves · One narrow antique-gold seam · Six shallow notches around the rim, one per Birth mark · One narrow brass tamper strip terminating in a tiny diamond seal · The seal remains intact in every Birth state · No handle large enough to become a second focal point

**Sticker:** Opaque warm-eggshell conservation label film · Matte mineral surface with permanent archival adhesive · Slight fibre or tooth, visible only at close range · Dark brown-black ink · Antique-gold micro-accent · Thin adhesive darkening at the perimeter · One microscopic trapped-air imperfection at most · Spot varnish over the six marks only · No paper curl, torn fibre or glossy vinyl shine · No conventional card drop shadow

**Contents:** One recognizable cucumber silhouette behind the left exposed glass band · One cropped cucumber form at lower right only if the jar fails recognition without it · One fine dill branch against a dark edge · Two or three mustard seeds · A few pale salt crystals settled in the foot · At most two visible bubbles at one time

## 8. Label master layout

The label is an archival identity record, not advertising.

```
BLUE ROOM PRESERVES                      REGISTER 01
THE BIRTH READING                                ✦
──────────────────────────────────────────────────
                 The Crowned Name
                      SUN SIGN
                    YEAR ANIMAL
                      LIFE PATH
                         RUNE
                       TRIGRAM
                      HEXAGRAM
                    ───────────
                   BY BIRTH ALONE
SEALED AT FIRST BREATH · NOT REVISED
```

**Typographic hierarchy**
- Institutional line: mono, 8–9px, wide tracking
- Product name: mono, 9–10px, small but definite
- Crowned name: display serif, 24–28px, primary title
- Six marks: mono, 9.5–10px, individually spaced, coloured as restrained material pigments
- Closing line: mono, 8–9px, quiet and final
- Register metadata: mono, 8px, subordinate and archival; never resembles a price, SKU or nutritional label
- Minimum text contrast: 4.5:1
- No line longer than approximately 24 characters inside the central label field
- Text remains flat; no curved baseline or optical distortion crosses the label

**Copy rules**
- Use THE BIRTH READING as the product name.
- Use BLUE ROOM PRESERVES only as institutional metadata.
- Preferred closing: SEALED AT FIRST BREATH · NOT REVISED.
- Never use pickle jokes.
- Never expose development labels such as M2.
- Never truncate the main explanatory paragraph with an accidental ellipsis.

## 9. Physical layer order

Correct compositing is critical to adhesion credibility.

1. Niche back wall
2. Jar rear glass
3. Rear cucumber and botanical silhouettes
4. Brine body and edge density
5. Front contents and mineral foot
6. Inner refraction and meniscus
7. Exterior sticker
8. Sticker ink and interactive mark regions
9. Sticker spot-varnish highlight
10. Foreground rim/shoulder reflections only on exposed glass
11. Lid and security seal

A broad glass reflection must never cross the opaque label. If it does, the label will appear to float inside the jar.

## 10. Interaction system

**Pointer response:** Reflection displacement 6–10px max · Base caustic displacement 2–4px · Contents parallax 1–2px · Label/card transform none · Jar transform none · Glass and liquid layers pointer-events:none

**Reflection law:** Shoulder highlight horizontal displacement up to 8px · Cool glass edge opacity change only · Base caustic displacement up to 3px · Contents maximum 1px parallax · Sticker varnish stationary · Sticker body stationary · Jar silhouette stationary

**Six-mark interaction.** Each printed mark is a real button. On hover or keyboard focus: ink darkens one step · a restrained material tint appears behind the word · its matching lid notch catches a restrained point of light · one small bubble releases from the adjacent brine for no more than 700ms · no line shifts position · focus remains visibly identifiable · only one notch and one bubble may be active at once · the original right-hand explanation and all surrounding menu state remain untouched.

**Birth/Tarot behavior.** Locked: the jar belongs to Birth only. Birth selected → labelled preserving jar. Tarot selected → a separate dry black-card exhibit occupies the same stage footprint. On transition the centre stage darkens, the current exhibit recedes, and the next resolves from the same fixed centre. Transition reuses the existing midpoint content swap and reduced-motion logic as its behavioral source of truth. The overall stage dimensions remain fixed, so columns never jump. The jar does not open, drain or pretend Tarot was preserved at birth.

**Idle motion.** Default completely still · bubbles only as a direct mark-selection response · no continuous wobble, shimmer, breathing or floating · motion stops when the page is not visible.

**Reduced motion.** No bubbles · no parallax · static reflection · immediate state swap or restrained crossfade · all content and controls remain available.

## 11. Main-menu integration

**Immutable original surface.** The jar is an addition inside the existing centre stage, not a redesign of the main menu. Left product and navigation column unchanged · right reading panel unchanged · Birth/Tarot selector unchanged in structure, position, appearance and behavior · global bottom navigation unchanged · three-column grid, gaps and alignment unchanged · existing copy outside the exhibit unchanged · existing routes, click targets, keyboard behavior and focus order unchanged · existing responsive stacking order unchanged.

**Allowed change boundary.** Replace only the visual object occupying the Birth face of the centre stage · add the jar/niche layers inside that stage's existing bounding box · preserve the production data-m2-* hooks and controller as the source of truth · keep all new CSS namespaced beneath `.birth-preserve` or `.pickle-jar` · the exhibit may respond internally to its six mark controls, but it cannot rewrite or restyle surrounding menu content · the jar must never overlap the application footer or require a surrounding column to move.

**Preservation test.** A comparison with the original menu must show zero visual or behavioral change outside the centre-stage exhibit. If any outside element shifts, resizes, restyles, rewrites or changes interaction, the integration fails.

## 13. Responsive law

**Desktop.** Maintain the existing three-column balance · jar remains centred between action and explanation columns · minimum visual gap between jar/niche and either side column 16px.

**Tablet.** Preserve the menu's existing responsive stacking order · preserve the complete jar silhouette · do not crop the lid or foot.

**Mobile.** Jar width `min(calc(100vw - 48px), 370px)` as a starting safety rule · minimum page-edge clearance 24px · keep the label readable without horizontal zoom · mark controls require visible keyboard focus and the largest practical touch regions without changing their printed appearance · preserve the menu's existing responsive order and surrounding spacing · no horizontal overflow, including scrollbar width.

## 14. Reflection and adversarial critique

**Primary risk: ordinary premium packaging.** The cream sticker, jar and dark lid can easily become an expensive condiment mock-up. The defence is archival hierarchy: no sales copy, no decorative brand crest, no wraparound marketing, no bright appetite photography and no excessive label border.

**Secondary risk: metaphor overwhelms product.** The delayed pickle recognition is the target. Immediate comedy is failure. Keep the vegetables dark and peripheral; the crowned name must be the first readable subject.

**Third risk: one metaphor forced across two products.** Birth and Tarot have opposite physical logics. Birth is fixed and preserved; Tarot is variable and drawn. The menu becomes richer when each receives an honest artifact rather than a shared novelty skin.

**Fourth risk: interaction breaks adhesion.** Any label tilt, float, displaced shadow or glass highlight crossing it destroys the sticker illusion. The label is physically attached and must behave like part of the jar.

**Fifth risk: material noise.** Too many scratches, bubbles, seeds or shifting highlights turn a restrained object into a visual-effects demonstration. Every material mark must explain glass, liquid, preservation or age.

## 15. Acceptance gates

The design is not final until every gate passes.

**Original-menu preservation gate.** Pixel comparison outside the centre-stage exhibit shows no layout, colour, typography or spacing change · left column, right panel, selector and global navigation retain their original hit areas and keyboard behavior · existing routes and Birth/Tarot state transitions pass unchanged · no original copy outside the exhibit is added, removed, truncated or rewritten.

**Silhouette gate.** In monochrome and at thumbnail size, observers identify a preserving jar rather than a capsule · sticker-removal test: with the label hidden, the silhouette and lid still identify a preserving jar.

**Hierarchy gate.** With the image blurred, the label is first, jar second, surrounding interface third · five-second test: the reading is noticed first and the pickle is discovered second.

**Adhesion gate.** The sticker reads as fixed to the exterior glass in still and interactive states.

**Taste gate.** No element suggests grocery marketing, farmhouse décor, comedy branding or laboratory horror · contents-removal test: with vegetables hidden, the label still reads unmistakably as Blue Room.

**Blue Room gate.** Blue-black remains the dominant room colour; warmth belongs to the reading.

**Physical-truth gate.** Meniscus is level, contents obey gravity, highlights respect material layer order and the jar does not float.

**Product-truth gate.** Birth language never says drawn; Tarot language never says preserved at birth.

**Interaction gate.** Six marks work by pointer and keyboard · the right read updates fully · glass never blocks a control · the label never moves.

**Responsive gate.** Desktop composition remains balanced · mobile has at least 24px edge clearance · no horizontal overflow or clipped lid, foot, label or selector.

**Motion gate.** The design remains complete with every animation disabled · no-effects test: with gradients, reflections and animation removed, silhouette, spacing and typography still carry the composition.

**Performance gate.** No perpetual high-cost filter loop · no canvas or WebGL dependency · idle state produces negligible continuous work.

## 16. Final master decision

Build the Birth Reading as a straight-sided preserving jar in a shallow blue-black niche, with a tall opaque cream conservation sticker adhered to the exterior, restrained olive-gold brine and one partially discovered pickle beside its edge.

The label is the reading. The jar is the preservation metaphor. The interface treats the object with complete seriousness.

The lid is dark enamel with six shallow seal notches and one narrow brass tamper strip. Selecting a mark illuminates its matching notch and releases one brief bubble; otherwise the jar is completely still. Reflections move, the jar and label do not.

Do not make Tarot another pickle jar. Give Tarot a dry black-card exhibit occupying the same fixed stage. The contrast between preserved Birth and drawn Tarot will make both products more understandable and the Blue Room world more materially coherent.

The existing main menu is not redesigned around this idea. Outside the centre-stage exhibit, it remains the original surface in layout, appearance, copy and behavior. The exhibit must earn its place inside the menu's existing geometry rather than asking the menu to accommodate it.

**Master line: Six marks, kept whole.**
