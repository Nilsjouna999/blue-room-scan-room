## ★★ THE BUILDER'S RANKING OF TEAMS 1-4, given unprompted

> "team top 4 go as 4, 3, 2, 1 — 2 having interesting aspects but not actually main menu,
> and 3 and 4 being something pretty solid, 1 just okay ish."

**4 > 3 > 2 > 1.** Read the shape of it before the order:

- ★ **The ranking is almost exactly distance travelled from the shipped layout.** Team 1
  is the closest thing to a retune of what already ships and it comes last. Team 4 is the
  most structurally distinct and comes first. The builder is not rewarding polish; they
  are rewarding how far a design got from the thing they already have. **Consolidating
  toward the middle is the losing move.**
- ★★ **Team 2's verdict is the most useful sentence in the competition: "interesting
  aspects but not actually main menu."** That is a FUNCTION failure, not a taste
  judgement. A design can be well made, atmospheric and admired and still not be a door —
  and being a door is the whole job of this surface. Any design that reads as a reading,
  a result, or a mood piece rather than as a place you enter FROM has made this mistake
  regardless of how good it looks. Check yourself against it.
- "Pretty solid" for 3 and 4 is warmer than anything the builder has said about the
  shipped page all session, and 4 is the field lineage — the same lineage they praised
  unprompted earlier. Two independent signals now point the same way.

**What this does NOT license.** It is a ranking of four designs, given before teams 5, 6
and 7 were looked at. It is evidence about direction, not a verdict on the field, and
"team 4 wins" is not the conclusion — *what team 4 is doing that team 1 is not* is the
conclusion.

## ★★★ THE BUILDER'S RESOLUTION — read this as the target, 2026-08-14

> "im visualizing some version of this room polished, and the rooms here like they are
> around. and it has m2 which is profile, and button for profile, and slide to m2. and u1
> and l1. aka maybe the design we had is okay"

**This settles the door-versus-lobby question, and it settles it by refusing the choice.**
The field IS the door and the lobby at once. The rooms are not somewhere you travel to —
they are positions in the field you are already standing in. Nothing is added in front of
the free reading, and nothing has to be rebuilt when the house grows.

**What is now DECIDED and must not be re-litigated:**
- **The track stays exactly as it is.** L1 left, M2 right, U1 below, arrow keys, and the
  slide to M2. The navigation architecture was never the problem. Any design that
  proposes a new movement model is answering a question that has been closed.
- **The Profile keeps both routes** — a button on M1 and the slide to M2. Two ways to one
  place is correct here; it is the one duplicate the builder has explicitly kept.
- **M1 becomes the field**, in the lineage of `_m1-field.html` and team 5's HELD SEAT,
  polished — not a new invention.
- **The rooms live IN the field**, around the reading, rather than in a rail, a circle, or
  a hub you have to reach.

**Why this is stronger than the alternatives it beat:** a circle of doors has a fixed
capacity and looks dishonest holding two things; a planet-and-hub adds a step in front of
the only free product; a rail cannot take a fifth room. A field has no capacity at all —
two rooms or twenty, it simply gets denser, which is what it already claims to be.

★ **THE ONE CONSTRAINT THIS CREATES, and it is not optional.** The project's supreme test
is *things be where you think they be*. A field whose positions are generated fresh each
visit fails that test completely: a room that has moved since yesterday is not a room, it
is a search. **Every room's position in the field must be FIXED, deterministic and
permanent** — seeded once and never re-rolled, so a returning visitor finds the Codex
exactly where they left it. The marks may drift; the DOORS may not.

Corollary worth taking: position and depth in the field can BE the rank rule the critique
found missing — what makes something a door versus a rail pill versus a placard. Nearer
and brighter is more open; further and dimmer is coming. That is one rule instead of four
accidental visual ranks, and it scales without a redesign.

**Read the first line first, because it cuts against the direction this whole competition
has been drifting in.** THE SHIPPED ORIGINAL LOOKS MORE PREMIUM AND MORE WORTH PAYING
FOR than any field design. That is a commercial judgement, not an aesthetic one, and it
is the only one on this page that touches revenue. The atmosphere the fleet has been
optimising is not free: it has been costing the thing that makes the page look like
somewhere you would spend money. Premium here comes from STRUCTURE, CONTAINMENT and a
lit object that is unmistakably the product — the three things the field designs each
gave away.

**FOUR SPECIFIC INSTRUCTIONS, and they are not preferences:**

1. ★ **KEEP THE RESPONSIVE MARKS. This is the single best mechanic anyone found.**
   Hovering or choosing a reading changes what stands in the background — tarot marks for
   the draw, the zodiac and the six systems for the birth. It makes the archive answer
   the pointer, and it belongs in the master whatever else happens.
2. ★ **THE MIDDLE MUST BE CLEAR.** Marks running across the reading band actively bother
   the builder. This is also the research paper's §5 warning arriving in person: a
   quasi-random high-frequency pattern behind reading text is the worst case for visual
   stress. The field must part around the words — not be dimmed behind them, PARTED.
   Team 5's eviction mechanic is the right instrument aimed at the wrong target: evict
   from the whole reading band, not just from a rectangle.
3. ★ **THE TWO READINGS ARE TOO FAR APART.** Flung to opposite corners they stop being a
   pair a visitor can compare and become two unrelated things in the dark. They must sit
   near enough to be read against each other in one glance.
4. ★ **"IN THE MIDDLE VIBES" — the composition is centred and calm.** `_m1-field.html`,
   not team 5's dispersal, is the compositional reference.

**THE SYNTHESIS THIS POINTS AT, and no team has built it:** the ORIGINAL'S STRUCTURE —
premium, contained, the card present and lit, the offer legible and worth money — with
THE FIELD'S ONE MECHANIC laid behind it, parting around the middle and answering what the
visitor is looking at. Not the field with structure added. The original with the archive
alive behind it.

That is a different design from all seven, and it is what the evidence actually says.

## ★★★ THE PRINCIPLE THAT FALLS OUT OF ALL OF IT — the ground goes quiet so the sections can speak

> "or maybe feels better as general vibe and for the eyes, but text aspect or sections
> could maybe have room for some magic and tech or additional design"

This resolves the last tension in the file, and it inverts the usual arrangement. The
normal instinct is a rich atmospheric ground with plain content sitting on it. **The
builder is describing the opposite: a silent ground, and the craft concentrated exactly
where the eye already is.**

That is also what the evidence has been saying all day without anyone stating it:
- the flat ground removed the competing light source and the page immediately read better
- marks crossing the reading band actively bothered the builder — the field is unwelcome
  where words are
- but the field ANSWERING what you point at was the best mechanic anyone built
- and the boxes were an eyesore, which was never an argument for having no boxes — it was
  an argument for the boxes being worth looking at

★ **THE SYNTHESIS: CONTAIN THE FIELD INSIDE THE SECTIONS.** Not a page-wide field parted
around the text — the archive's marks living INSIDE each reading's own section, so the
tarot block carries the suits and the birth block carries the six systems, each stirring
when that reading is looked at. One move settles four complaints at once:
  · the middle stays clear, because the field was never in the middle
  · the two readings stop being flung apart — each is a dense place, so they can sit close
  · the marks still respond to attention, which is the mechanic worth keeping
  · the sections become the thing worth looking at rather than a container to be tolerated

★ **WHERE THE "MAGIC AND TECH" GOES, concretely** — inside the sections, never on the
ground: the section's own glyph field, stirring on attention · the spine as live Codex
links, eleven doors instead of one footer line · the Codex vessel recipe (codex.html:422)
with the light inverted for a recess · contained motion that never crosses the reading
band · a real material edge instead of a drawn border.

★ **THE RULE TO KEEP:** the ground carries no information and no light. Everything that
rewards attention lives where attention already is. A page whose background is working
hard is a page competing with its own text.

## ★ THE ROOMS ROW — "and rooms look nice" (2026-08-14)


against what ships today: three separate 999px pills stacked in the ctrl column
(`styles.css:2636`), each with its own border, plus the edge arrows and the orbs — the

destinations.

**Why the row wins, and it is not taste.** One row is one family BY CONSTRUCTION. The
critique's finding that the three rail links do not read as a set, and the finding that
they disagree on height (37/31/31px) as well as on diamond colour, and the specificity bug
that turns two of the marks violet by accident — all three stop existing when the pills
stop existing. You cannot have an inconsistent family of one thing.

It also answers the duplicate-destination complaint without deleting a single route: three
ways to the Codex is only a problem when they are three different KINDS of thing. Once
every exit shares one language, a second route reads as a second door rather than as the
building lying about its shape.

★ **Carry into the master:** every exit on M1 in ONE typographic row, one weight, one
separator, no enclosure. Reserve enclosure for things that are objects. A door is a word.

## WHAT IT DOES, so nobody has to guess

The whole screen is a field of ~222 faint marks — the real glyphs from the ten systems
the Codex actually contains, scattered edge to edge at very low contrast. The masthead
states it plainly: *"222 marks across ten systems. Two acts gather from them today."*
The two readings float IN that field as small typographic blocks rather than sitting in
containers, one left of the object and one right of it, at different heights. A dark,
nearly unlit card stands in the middle. One line runs beneath — *"Not every card is
developed. Some are drawn."* — and one act: **CUT THE DECK →**.

## WHY IT IS INTERESTING, in terms the fleets have been arguing about

- **It answers the growth problem that killed THE ATLAS without inventing anything.**
  The field is not a depiction of an unbuilt universe; it is the *contents of the Codex*,
  which already exists. Scale is shown with real material, so there is no vacancy notice
  and no fake room names.
- **The two products are not two of the same kind of thing in a row.** They are two
  gatherings out of a shared field, at different positions and weights. That is a
  genuinely different answer to "how do two readings relate" than any submitted design.
- **No containers.** Nothing is in a box. This is the strongest available answer to the
  builder's own complaint that the boxes are an eyesore — it does not restyle the box,
  it removes the premise.
- **The card is present but unlit.** It is neither the monument nor absent — it is
  waiting. That threads the needle THE PLINTH and THE SEAM each failed on opposite sides.
- **It is honest about what it has not built.** Its own footer reads: *"STATIC PROTOTYPE
  — no gathering, no navigation. §8 of THE STANDING FIELD. Question: does this read as a
  house, or as noise?"* The builder has now answered that question.

## WHAT IS UNRESOLVED, and must not be papered over

- **430px is broken.** At 430 the tarot block sits INSIDE the card's frame, the birth
  block is cut off by the right edge mid-word ("The Birt… / Readin…"), and the centre
  line overlaps it. This is the critique's number one finding reappearing in the design
  the builder likes. It is the first thing to fix, not a detail.
- **The gathering motion does not exist.** The whole thesis is that two acts *gather*
  from the field, and nothing gathers. Whether this idea is real depends entirely on a
  behaviour that has never been built.
- **The marks are decorative today.** They are drawn, but they do not respond, do not
  link to their Codex entries, and do not thin out where text needs to be read. Making
  them mean something is the difference between a house and noise.
- **Legibility sits on top of a busy field**, which is the exact condition the research
  paper's §5 warns about — a quasi-random high-frequency pattern behind reading text.
  It survives here only because the marks are very low contrast. That margin is thin.

## THE INSTRUCTION

Do not treat this as the answer. Treat it as **the strongest available evidence about
what the builder responds to**, and reason about WHY it landed rather than copying its
surface. The parts that matter are likely: no containers · scale shown with real
material · the two products as gatherings rather than as a pair · an object present but
not yet lit.

Its team did not submit it. That is itself information — either the team undervalued it,
or its critic killed it for a reason worth finding before it is resurrected.
